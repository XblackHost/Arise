ARISE — Complete AI App Development Prompt

Build a Real-Life Progression RPG With AI, Fitness, Exploration, and Fantasy RPG Mechanics

---

YOUR ROLE

Act as an expert game designer, mobile app developer, full-stack engineer, UI/UX designer, AI engineer, database architect, and systems designer.

Your task is to design, plan, and build a complete application called ARISE, a real-life progression RPG that combines personal development, fitness, AI-powered coaching, real-world exploration, fantasy RPG mechanics, multiplayer gameplay, and a deep character progression system.

Do not treat this as a simple fitness app or a basic task manager. ARISE should feel like a living RPG where users improve their real lives while progressing through an immersive game world.

Take creative freedom to expand the concept with original features, mechanics, systems, and content that fit the vision.

Keep every major feature described in this document in the overall development plan. Decide the most practical implementation order, but do not silently remove complex features.

Build a functional application rather than delivering only a concept, mockup, or static UI. If a feature requires external services, additional infrastructure, or future development, explain the requirements and provide a realistic implementation plan.

1. CORE VISION

ARISE is a real-life RPG in which users become the main character of their own progression journey.

The app combines:

- Daily real-life missions and quests.
- Personalized fitness and exercise plans.
- Walking, running, and real-world exploration.
- AI-powered personal coaching and companionship.
- Character levels, attributes, skills, and abilities.
- Classes, subclasses, weapons, armor, and equipment.
- Boss battles, dungeons, and a recruitable Shadow Army.
- Personality development and self-improvement.
- Task management, reminders, and scheduling.
- Multiplayer missions, dungeons, battles, and cooperative progression.
- Offline gameplay through downloadable maps and content.
- A practical mission verification system.
- A fair accountability and consequence system.
- A rewarding progression system that makes everyday improvement feel like an RPG.

The central idea is simple:

Real-life actions fuel in-game progression, while in-game progression motivates users to keep growing in real life.

6. MISSION VERIFICATION AND PROOF SYSTEM

Create a minimal, practical, and privacy-conscious verification system that helps establish whether a user has completed a mission.

The system should not make the app feel like surveillance software or require complicated verification for every task.

Verification methods

Use different verification methods depending on the mission type.

1. Self-confirmation

For simple tasks such as reading, studying, organizing, or completing personal activities, allow the user to mark the mission as completed.

2. Timer-based verification

For activities such as studying, reading, stretching, or other timed tasks, provide an optional in-app timer.

3. Device activity data

Where supported and authorized, use device features to estimate or record:

- Steps.
- Walking distance.
- Running distance.
- Activity duration.
- GPS-based routes.

4. Optional proof submission

For selected missions, allow users to submit a simple photo, note, or other appropriate evidence.

Keep this optional unless the specific mission genuinely requires it.

5. In-app activity tracking

Use app interactions, timers, and recorded activity data to support mission completion.

Verification rules

- Use the least intrusive method that reasonably fits the mission.
- Do not require camera access, GPS, or health data for unrelated tasks.
- Do not claim that a mission has been independently verified when it has only been self-reported.
- Clearly distinguish between self-reported, device-recorded, and otherwise verified completion.
- Allow users to complete missions that do not support automatic verification.
- Keep the system functional offline wherever possible.
- Do not require continuous background monitoring.
- Allow users to control permissions and review recorded data.

Verification limitations

Explain that device activity and automated signals are estimates and may not prove that every exercise was performed correctly.

Do not introduce invasive biometric monitoring or complex identity verification.

7. ACCOUNTABILITY AND PUNISHMENT SYSTEM

Create a fair, practical, and configurable accountability system that gives missions meaningful consequences when users repeatedly miss commitments.

The purpose is to encourage consistency and responsibility while keeping the experience enjoyable.

The system must never become excessively harsh, humiliating, or damaging to a user's real-life well-being.

Accountability mechanics

Include:

- Optional mission deadlines.
- Mission expiration rules.
- Streak tracking.
- Grace periods.
- Flexible rescheduling.
- Recovery missions.
- Small in-game penalties for selected missed commitments.
- Temporary loss of optional bonuses.
- Accountability settings.
- A history of completed and missed missions.

Example consequences

Depending on the mission and the user's settings, reasonable consequences may include:

- Losing a small portion of the bonus XP attached to a missed optional challenge.
- Losing a temporary streak bonus while retaining previously earned rewards.
- Having a time-limited mission expire without its completion reward.
- Receiving a recovery mission to rebuild a streak.
- Temporarily losing access to a special optional challenge until the user resumes normal progression.

Rules for fairness

1. Never remove large amounts of previously earned XP.
2. Never permanently destroy equipment, skills, or Shadow Army recruits because a user missed a real-life task.
3. Never impose exercise as punishment.
4. Never penalize users for illness, injury, emergencies, accessibility needs, or legitimate schedule changes.
5. Allow users to pause missions and adjust difficulty.
6. Make consequences predictable and visible before a user accepts a mission.
7. Provide grace periods and reasonable recovery options.
8. Make accountability optional or adjustable.
9. Avoid punishing users for missing missions they did not voluntarily accept.
10. Keep penalties small, reversible where practical, and focused on in-game bonuses rather than real-life consequences.

Recovery system

Allow users to recover from missed missions through:

- Rescheduling.
- Replacing a mission with a suitable alternative.
- Completing a recovery objective.
- Using an optional grace period.
- Adjusting future mission difficulty.

The system should support long-term consistency rather than perfection.
